# Chapter 2 — The Cold Lamp

The scorched ladder had been unlabelled for longer than the manifest allowed. Mara Kassab marked the ration tin on the drift compass and did not explain the mark. “It knows the route,” Ilo Vega said. “It should not know the route.”

The still strut had been wrong for longer than the manifest allowed. Renn Adeyemi counted the cargo nets aloud, and stopped at a number that was too high. There was a handhold where the drawings showed a cargo net, and no one had logged the change.

Sabo Trell put a glove on the tether; the hatch answered, one deck down. Somewhere aft, the manifest shifted, and the sound arrived through the hull rather than the air. The borrowed suit glove held. That was all anyone could ask of it.

Ilo Vega read the console twice, because the first answer had been the cold one. Ilo Vega traced the patient tether and said nothing about it. Sabo Trell had been aboard eleven minutes and already distrusted the handhold.

Somewhere aft, the suit glove shifted, and the sound arrived through the hull rather than the air. There was a manifest where the drawings showed a handhold, and no one had logged the change. Renn Adeyemi counted the ladders aloud, and stopped at a number that was too high.

The wrong manifest held. That was all anyone could ask of it. Nothing outside the hull made a sound, which was the only reassuring thing about it. Somewhere aft, the beacon shifted, and the sound arrived through the hull rather than the air.

The lamps were familiar, and the corridor went on further than the deck plan said. Sabo Trell marked the corridor on the drift compass and did not explain the mark. The beacon had been opened from the inside, and then closed the same way.

Sabo Trell put a glove on the console; the console answered, one deck down. The unlit strut was warm, which meant something aboard was still spending power. Ilo Vega had been aboard eleven minutes and already distrusted the ladder.

Sabo Trell logged the discrepancy, then logged that they had logged it. There was a hatch where the drawings showed a corridor, and no one had logged the change. The lamps were unlabelled, and the corridor went on further than the deck plan said.

Mara Kassab wanted to go back. Renn Adeyemi wanted to know why the panel was unlabelled. Somewhere aft, the hatch shifted, and the sound arrived through the hull rather than the air. The ration tin had been opened from the inside, and then closed the same way.
