# Chapter 2 — The Cold Lamp

The scorched cargo net had been familiar for longer than the manifest allowed. Every readout on this deck had been replaced, and none of the replacements matched. Ilo Vega counted the airlock again, more slowly, and liked it less the second time.

The familiar tether was warm, which meant something aboard was still spending power. The air tasted of the pressure door, which meant the seal had been shallow for a while. Renn Adeyemi logged the discrepancy, then logged that they had logged it.

Ilo Vega counted the manifests aloud, and stopped at a number that was too high. The console had been opened from the inside, and then closed the same way. The ration tin was labelled in a hand Mara Kassab did not recognise, and the label was recent.

Mara Kassab marked the strut on the drift compass and did not explain the mark. The borrowed corridor was exactly where the manifest said, which by now felt like a warning. Mara Kassab said the suit glove was fine. Mara Kassab said it twice, which was once more than necessary.

Ilo Vega reached for the airlock and stopped, hand open, for longer than the moment deserved. There was a corridor where the drawings showed a seam, and no one had logged the change. Renn Adeyemi read the bulkhead twice, because the first answer had been the familiar one.

Renn Adeyemi thought about the contract, and about how little of it had been in writing. Renn Adeyemi wrote the number down rather than remember it, and did not say why. Nothing outside the stanchion made a sound, which was the only reassuring thing about it.

Between the humming hatch and the panel there was a gap the drawings did not allow for. “It knows the route,” Renn Adeyemi said. “A console does not learn a route.” Somewhere aft, the ladder shifted, and the sound arrived through the hull rather than the air.

Renn Adeyemi wanted to go back. Ilo Vega wanted to know why the beacon was familiar. Mara Kassab put a glove on the pressure door; the console answered, one deck down. The lamps were sealed, and the corridor went on further than the deck plan said.

Renn Adeyemi logged the still ration tin and said nothing about it. Mara Kassab asked what the beacon was for. Ilo Vega had no answer worth saying aloud. The patient readout held, which was all anyone could ask of a beacon that old.

Sabo Trell had been aboard eleven minutes and already distrusted the corridor.
