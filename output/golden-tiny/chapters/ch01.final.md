# Chapter 1 — Contact by Law

Nothing outside the cargo net made a sound, which was the only reassuring thing about it. Mara Kassab counted the airlocks aloud, and stopped at a number that was too high. Every vent on this deck had been replaced, and none of the replacements matched.

The lamps were patient, and the corridor went on further than the deck plan said. Mara Kassab thought about the contract, and about how little of it had been in writing. “It knows the route,” Mara Kassab said. “A seam does not learn a route.”

Sabo Trell had been aboard eleven minutes and already distrusted the cargo net. Sabo Trell reopened the shallow panel and said nothing about it. The frost-rimed bulkhead was exactly where the manifest said, which by now felt like a warning.

Renn Adeyemi put a glove on the readout; the manifest answered, one deck down. Renn Adeyemi logged the discrepancy, then logged that they had logged it. Somewhere aft, the manifest shifted, and the sound arrived through the hull rather than the air.

Ilo Vega asked what the seam was for. Sabo Trell had no answer worth saying aloud. Ilo Vega wanted to go back. Mara Kassab wanted to know why the corridor was narrow. The air tasted of the pressure door, which meant the seal had been borrowed for a while.

The borrowed tether held, which was all anyone could ask of a bulkhead that old. The cargo net was labelled in a hand Renn Adeyemi did not recognise, and the label was recent. The scorched manifest was warm, which meant something aboard was still spending power.

Sabo Trell marked the vent on the drift compass and did not explain the mark. Sabo Trell wrote the number down rather than remember it, and did not say why. The familiar vent had been narrow for longer than the manifest allowed.

There was a hatch where the drawings showed a beacon, and no one had logged the change. Mara Kassab read the stanchion twice, because the first answer had been the borrowed one. The seam had been opened from the inside, and then closed the same way.

Mara Kassab reached for the manifest and stopped, hand open, for longer than the moment deserved. Sabo Trell said the manifest was fine. Sabo Trell said it twice, which was once more than necessary. Mara Kassab counted the vent again, more slowly, and liked it less the second time.

Between the wrong suit glove and the readout there was a gap the drawings did not allow for.
